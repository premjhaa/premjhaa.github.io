Meet Prem Jha

01

Prem Jha is an Indian author and emerging writer based in Chandigarh, India. He is the author of Pages I Never Sent, his debut book exploring love, memories, solitude, relationships, and the emotions that often remain unspoken.

His writing is shaped by an interest in human psychology, philosophy, personal experiences, and the gap between what people express publicly and what they carry privately. Alongside writing, Prem is pursuing a bachelor's degree in Tourism and Travel Management and is interested in travel, culture, digital media, technology, and creative entrepreneurship.

More about Prem

As a young independent author, he is building a multidisciplinary creative identity that brings together literature, digital storytelling, communication, and contemporary forms of publishing. Through his writing and future projects, Prem aims to explore human experiences from different perspectives while developing a distinctive voice of his own.

The debut

Pages I Never Sent

02

Pages I Never Sent is a collection born from the spaces between words - the letters left unsaid, the feelings hidden too well, and the goodbyes that never found their voice. Each page is a quiet confession, a memory, a wound, and a prayer.

This book is for anyone who has felt emotions deeply, lost silently, overthought endlessly, and written in the dark just to stay sane. These pages are not answers. They are pieces of the heart and mind he had to let go of.

If you've ever written something you never sent, this book is for you.

Some feelings need
a little space.

A feeling. A thought. Something you wish you had said.

Leave your name out. Your note goes to Prem's private inbox, not a public wall.

Please leave out names, contact details, or anything that could identify you or someone else. This is not a counselling or emergency service. A reply is not promised.

Your note goes privately to Prem via Web3Forms, which processes message and technical data such as IP/browser details. No name or email is requested, but this is not complete anonymity. Provider message records expire after seven days; inbox emails may remain. Your words will not be published or reused without separate permission.

No name. No email. Your words only.
